Mutual Non-Disclosure

Before we get into it, let's both be covered.

When we sit down, you are going to tell me things about your business you would not put in an email. Your real numbers, where your process leaks, what you have tried that did not work. And I am going to show you how I build, not just what I build. Neither one of us should have to talk around anything. So we sign first, and it runs both directions.

What this actually does

Plain English, no lawyer voice. Three things, and they apply to me exactly as much as they apply to you.

01

Your business stays yours

Whatever you tell me about your pipeline, your margins, your customers, or your plans does not leave the room. It does not go to another client, another partner, or another venture. Not in a case study, not as an example on a call.

02

My build stays mine

To show you whether this fits, I have to open the hood. The automations, the prompts, the architecture, the sequence. That know-how is worth something because it is not public. You agree not to hand it to someone else to rebuild.

03

Nobody is committed to anything

This is a confidentiality agreement and nothing else. It does not hire me, it does not obligate you, and it does not create any referral or fee arrangement. If we work together, that gets its own separate written agreement.

Pick the one that fits you

Two versions. Same protection in both directions either way. The difference is whether your industry has a compliance team that needs specific language on the page. Both are blank templates, so fill in your name and company when you sign.

Standard

Mutual NDA

For most businesses. Contractors, home services, medical and dental practices, agencies, e-commerce, professional services. If you are not in a licensed financial or real estate role, this is your document.

  • Covers both sides equally. Same obligations on me as on you.
  • Protects your customer data specifically. I never market to it, sell it, or reuse it.
  • Two-year term, three-year confidentiality tail.
  • California law, Los Angeles County venue.

Mutual NDA

For lenders, mortgage originators, title, escrow, and real estate brokerages. Everything in the standard version, plus the compliance language your legal team is going to look for before they let you sign.

  • States in writing that it creates no referral arrangement, no marketing services agreement, and no exchange of anything of value.
  • Nothing in it requires either of us to act against a licensing obligation or an employer's policy.

How to sign it

Takes about two minutes. Use whatever signing tool you already have. If you do not have one, print it and sign it, a photo of the signed page is fine.

Download

Grab the version that fits your business. It is a standard PDF.

Upload

Drop it into DocuSign, Dropbox Sign, Adobe, or whatever your team uses.

Fill and sign

Date at the top, your name and company in the blank, signature at the bottom.

Send it back

Email the signed copy over. I countersign and send you the fully executed version.

Read this part too

These are working templates. They were not drafted by an attorney and they have not been reviewed by counsel. I use them because a plain, mutual, readable agreement beats no agreement, not because they are bulletproof.

If your business has counsel, run it past them before you sign. If they want changes, send them over. I would rather sign your redline than have you sign something you have not had looked at. And if you are in a licensed role, regulated or otherwise, your own compliance obligations always come first.
