What was happening under the hood

The visible part is the SMS thread. The invisible part is everything the platform did in parallel.

At 6:42:00 (the missed call)

  • Platform detects unanswered ring-out.
  • Caller ID captured (Maria's number).
  • CRM lookup runs. No prior contact. New lead.
  • MCTB campaign trigger fires.
  • Message generation: AI fills in business name, signs as Sarah, applies the trained tone.
  • SMS sent through 10DLC channel from the business's registered number.

At 6:43:08 (customer first reply)

  • Reply lands in the unified inbox.
  • AI parses the message: detects symptom ("blowing warm, humming"), interprets urgency (high but not emergency), categorizes as HVAC service call.
  • AI checks knowledge base for diagnostic flow: capacitor or compressor likely.
  • Routing rule applies: not an emergency, route to standard scheduling flow.

At 6:43:51 (zip code captured)

  • Zip code 91350 cross-referenced against service area map. Match.
  • Calendar integration queries next available slot. Tomorrow 7 AM is open.
  • Pricing logic pulled: service call $89, applied to repair, no emergency surcharge for morning appointments.

At 6:45:32 (booking confirmed)

  • Calendar slot reserved for tomorrow 7 AM.
  • CRM contact created with name, address, phone, problem description, source (MCTB), urgency tag, customer-history flag (new).
  • Pipeline stage assigned: "Booked - Awaiting Service."
  • Confirmation SMS fired with appointment details and TCPA-compliant STOP keyword.
  • Owner Slack-style notification queued for the next-morning brief: "1 new MCTB booking · HVAC · Saugus · 7 AM · $89 service call."
  • Tech assignment workflow triggered for the morning routing.

The patterns inside every $4K SMS thread

Across hundreds of these threads, four patterns repeat.

1. Speed at the top of the funnel

The 11-second first SMS is what catches the caller before they Google your competitor. Speed at this single moment matters more than anything else in the entire SMS sequence.

2. Symptom-to-diagnosis hint in message 2

"That sounds like a capacitor or compressor issue, both fixable" calms the panic and signals competence. Customers commit faster to a service that already named their problem.

3. Service area qualified before slot offer

The zip code question (asked early) prevents wasting a slot offer on a customer outside your coverage. It also costs zero conversion because customers expect it.

4. Booking offered as a one-question commit

"Soonest slot is tomorrow at 7 AM, want me to lock it in?" Yes/no commit. Friction stays low. Most callers say yes because they want the appointment, not a list of options to pick from.
